Three examples, every number

Three small matrices, each seen from every angle: the matrix, the two graphs, the matching and the chain of values. Every number is computed in the browser.

In this section: a case in which the lower bound is exact, one in which it is not, and an example you can edit freely.

How to read an example

$\Gamma$ is a symmetric nonnegative $n\times n$ matrix, and $\gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_{ii}$ is its trace. For each example we show four panels:

  • the matrix $\Gamma$ (diagonal in amber, off-diagonal in blue);
  • the AWCD bordered graph: root $r$, edges $(r,i)$ of weight $\Gamma_{ii}$, edges $\{i,j\}$ of weight $\Gamma_{ij}$;
  • the graph with loops and the optimal pseudoforest $F^*$ (one loop $\ell_i$ of weight $\Gamma_{ii}$ per vertex);
  • the gain matching: the edge $\{i,j\}$ assigned to the vertex $v\in\{i,j\}$ gains $(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{vv})^+$; $M$ is the maximum value.

Below the panels comes the chain

$$\Efull(\Gamma)\ \ge\ \LB=\val(R)-\gamma=w(F^*)-\gamma=M.$$

Here $\Efull(\Gamma)$ is the minimum error $\sum_{i\le j}|\Gamma_{ij}-H_{ij}|$ with $H=\sum_U p_UX_U$, $p\ge0$ and $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$ ($\one_U$ is the indicator vector of $U\subseteq V=\{1,\dots,n\}$). $\val(R)$ is the value of the one-sided relaxation and $w(F^*)$ the weight of $F^*$. Only the first relation can be strict.

1 Example 1

A 2×2 case in which the bound is exact

$\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\3&2\end{pmatrix}$. The off-diagonal value exceeds both diagonal values: something is bound to be lost.

The matching assigns the edge $\{1,2\}$ to vertex $1$, with gain $3-1=2$. A decomposition with error $2$ exists, so the bound is attained.

Two optimal decompositions, with their errors
The matching certificate proves optimality

The AWCD matching bound turns the pair “edge $\{1,2\}$ → vertex $1$” into the dual vector $y_{12}=+1$, $y_{11}=-1$ (all others $0$).

It satisfies $-1\le y\le 1$ and, for every $U\ne\emptyset$, $\sum_{i\le j,\ i,j\in U}y_{ij}\le0$. Its value is $\sum_{i\le j}\Gamma_{ij}y_{ij}=3-1=2$, which is exactly $\Efull(\Gamma)$.

2 Example 2

A 3×3 case: equal bounds, but not the optimum

$\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&1&0\\2&0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Here the pseudoforest lower bound and the matching bound are both $2$, but the minimum error is $3$.

$F^*$ contains the edge $\{1,3\}$ (weight $2$) and the loops $\ell_2,\ell_3$ (weight $1$ each): $w(F^*)=4$ and $\LB=4-2=2$. The matching assigns $\{1,3\}$ to vertex $1$ with gain $2$.

The central message

$\LB=M$ is a theorem: the two lower bounds always coincide. But this equality does not say that the bound is exact. Here $\Efull(\Gamma)=3\gt 2$.

Two optimal decompositions (error 3) and the Stage B choice

The all-ones matrix $\one\one^\top=X_{\{1,2,3\}}$ has error $3$ and trace $3$. The diagonal matrix $\mathrm{diag}(0,1,1)=X_{\{2\}}+X_{\{3\}}$ also has error $3$, but trace $2$.

The optimum is not unique. AWCD Stage B, which minimizes the trace $\tr H=\sum_U|U|p_U$ among the optima, picks $p_{\{2\}}=p_{\{3\}}=1$.

Dual certificate: why $3$ really is the minimum

The dual of $\Efull$ asks for $y$ with $-1\le y_{ij}\le1$ and $\sum_{i\le j,\ i,j\in U}y_{ij}\le0$ for every $U\ne\emptyset$; its value is $\sum_{i\le j}\Gamma_{ij}y_{ij}$. Take $y_{11}=-1$, $y_{12}=1$, $y_{13}=1$, $y_{23}=-1$, all others $0$.

The matching certificate ($y_{13}=1$, $y_{11}=-1$) is worth only $2$: it offsets every $+1$ with a $-1$ on the diagonal. Here, instead, the $+1$ on $y_{12}$ is offset by $y_{23}=-1$, which is off-diagonal and free because $\Gamma_{23}=0$.

Rank-one matrices

The “rank-one” button generates $\Gamma=bb^\top$, with $b$ a vector of nonnegative integers. A recent result (October 2026, still to be checked against the literature) states that the bound is exact on these matrices: $\Efull(bb^\top)=\LB$.

Every time you press the button, the site also checks it on 300 random vectors $b$.

Key takeaways
  • $\LB=\val(R)-\gamma=w(F^*)-\gamma=M$ always holds: three ways of computing the same number.
  • The bound can be exact (Example 1, matrices in the cone, $bb^\top$) or not (Example 2: $2\lt 3$).
  • Equality between the two lower bounds says nothing about their exactness.
  • The optimum of $\Efull$ need not be unique; AWCD Stage B picks the one with minimum trace.
