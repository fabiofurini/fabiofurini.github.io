Positive and negative errors

A reconstruction $H$ can err in two directions: giving too little or giving too much. The pseudoforest relaxation treats the two directions differently, and the difference depends on where the cell is: on the diagonal or off it.

In this chapter: underestimate and overestimate; why a diagonal underestimate can always be removed; how to go from the full error $\Efull$ to the one-sided relaxation $(R)$; why the lower bound is $\LB=\val(R)-\gamma$ (with $\val(R)$ the optimal value of $(R)$) and not $\val(R)$.

Two kinds of error

Fix a reconstruction $H=H(p)=\sum_U p_U X_U$, with $p_U\ge0$ and $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$ (Chapter B). For each cell $i\le j$ of the upper triangle we compare $\Gamma_{ij}$ with $H_{ij}$.

Definition

With $(a)^+=\max\{0,a\}$ we define the underestimate $s^+_{ij}$ and the overestimate $s^-_{ij}$:

$$s^+_{ij}=(\Gamma_{ij}-H_{ij})^+,\qquad s^-_{ij}=(H_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij})^+.$$

Thus $H_{ij}+s^+_{ij}-s^-_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}$, and at most one of the two slacks is positive.

Underestimate: $H_{ij}<\Gamma_{ij}$, i.e. a positive error $\Gamma_{ij}-H_{ij}>0$. Overestimate: $H_{ij}>\Gamma_{ij}$, i.e. a negative error. The error of the cell is $s^+_{ij}+s^-_{ij}=|\Gamma_{ij}-H_{ij}|$.

We use the guide's reference example, with trace $\gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_{ii}=0+1+1=2$:

$$\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&1&0\\2&0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

From the full problem to $(R)$ in five steps

  1. Full problem. The variables are $p\ge0$ and the slacks $s^\pm\ge0$; every slack is paid.
    $$\begin{aligned}\Efull(\Gamma)=\min\ & \sum_{i\le j}\bigl(s^+_{ij}+s^-_{ij}\bigr)\\ \text{s.t. }\ & \sum_{U\supseteq\{i,j\}}p_U+s^+_{ij}-s^-_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}\quad (i\le j),\qquad p,s^\pm\ge0.\end{aligned}$$
    For $i=j$ the sum is over $U\ni i$; “s.t.” reads “subject to”.
  2. No underestimate on the diagonal. We may assume $s^+_{ii}=0$: a diagonal underestimate is removed by increasing $p_{\{i\}}$, the coefficient of the singleton $\{i\}$, and the error does not increase. The diagonal constraint becomes
    $$\sum_{U\ni i}p_U\ \ge\ \Gamma_{ii},\qquad s^-_{ii}=\sum_{U\ni i}p_U-\Gamma_{ii}.$$
    Why the error does not increase

    Let $\delta=s^+_{ii}>0$. The factor $X_{\{i\}}$ has a single $1$, in the cell $(i,i)$. Setting $p_{\{i\}}\leftarrow p_{\{i\}}+\delta$ and $s^+_{ii}\leftarrow0$ keeps all constraints satisfied and changes only the cell $(i,i)$. The error drops by exactly $\delta$. This is the “Fix with singletons” button in the figure.

  3. The diagonal pays the excess trace. Summing over $i$, each $U$ appears $|U|$ times: $\sum_i s^-_{ii}=\sum_U|U|\,p_U-\gamma$. The diagonal overestimate is still paid, in $(R)$ as well.
  4. Off the diagonal, the overestimate becomes free. Remove $s^-_{ij}$ ($i<j$) from the objective. The constraint becomes an inequality, and the underestimate $q_{ij}=s^+_{ij}$ is still paid:
    $$\begin{aligned}(R)\quad \min\ & \sum_{U}|U|\,p_U+\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}\\ \text{s.t. }\ & \sum_{U\supseteq\{i,j\}}p_U+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}\quad (i<j),\\ & \sum_{U\ni i}p_U\ge\Gamma_{ii}\quad (i\in V),\qquad p,q\ge0.\end{aligned}$$
  5. The bound. Step 4 can only lower the minimum, and the constant $\gamma$ from step 3 does not appear in $(R)$. With $\val(R)$ the optimal value of $(R)$, the lower bound is
    $$\LB=\val(R)-\gamma\ \le\ \Efull(\Gamma).$$
Why subtract the trace

$\val(R)$ includes the coverage of the diagonal, $\sum_U|U|p_U\ge\gamma$, which is not an error. For $\Gamma=\one\one^\top$ (all ones, $n=3$) the reconstruction is exact, $\Efull=0$, but $\val(R)=3$.

In the reference example $\val(R)=4>\Efull(\Gamma)=3$, while $\LB=4-2=2\le3$.

Key takeaways
  • Underestimate $s^+_{ij}$: $H_{ij}<\Gamma_{ij}$. Overestimate $s^-_{ij}$: $H_{ij}>\Gamma_{ij}$.
  • On the diagonal, an underestimate is removed with the singleton $p_{\{i\}}$: we may impose $\sum_{U\ni i}p_U\ge\Gamma_{ii}$.
  • In $(R)$ the diagonal overestimate is still paid ($\sum_U|U|p_U-\gamma$); the off-diagonal overestimate is free; the off-diagonal underestimate $q_{ij}$ is paid.
  • $\LB=\val(R)-\gamma\le\Efull(\Gamma)$; subtracting $\gamma$ is essential.
