How do you build a benchmark for quadratic optimization?

QPLIB: from thousands of heterogeneous candidate instances to a structured, challenging and reproducible quadratic programming library.

THE CENTRAL INSIGHT

A benchmark must do more than collect files. QPLIB defines a taxonomy, a selection principle based on computational challenge, and a common data format for very different kinds of quadratic programs.

01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

What makes this problem challenging?

Quadratic programming includes continuous, mixed-integer, convex and nonconvex models with quadratic objectives and/or constraints. Existing benchmarks covered parts of this landscape, making comparisons across algorithmic communities difficult.

02 / THE IDEA

From problem structure to an algorithm

01Gather

Collect candidate models from community submissions and existing optimization libraries.

02Classify

Use a structured taxonomy distinguishing the mathematical properties of instances.

03Select and publish

Retain a manageable and varied set of challenging examples in a documented exchange format.

03 / ORIGINAL CONTRIBUTIONS

What does this work add?

A broad QP taxonomy

A consistent classification scheme that makes very different quadratic programs comparable.

Benchmark-selection methodology

A principled way to screen out broadly easy models without discarding instances that remain difficult for many solver families.

Reusable dataset and format

A documented library of optimization instances with a common file representation.

Community resource

A cross-disciplinary foundation for future solver benchmarking rather than a claim that one algorithm wins.

04 / REPORTED FINDINGS

What does the study demonstrate?

The authors report collecting more than 8,000 candidate instances before the filtering process. Their stated objective is to retain a diverse and challenging library; the article explicitly does not aim to rank quadratic-programming solvers.

Based on the original QPLIB manuscript, including its introduction, benchmark-construction section, and file-format appendix. No unsupported numerical comparison has been added.

06 / SUBSEQUENT RESEARCH

How other researchers have used this work

A selection of citing studies whose use of this contribution can be identified from the published paper.

A Practical and Optimal First-Order Method for Large-Scale Convex Quadratic Programming ↗

The authors use QPLIB as an external benchmark: they extract 33 convex QP instances from the collection and combine them with the Maros–Mészáros set to evaluate their first-order method. They also explain why larger synthetic instances are needed beyond existing libraries.

Les Cahiers du GERAD, G–2025–68 ↗

The study uses three 0–1 quadratic-programming instances from QPLIB in its experimental campaign. QPLIB thus provides test cases beyond the instances generated specifically for the study.
