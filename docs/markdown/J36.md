# Casting Light on the Hidden Bilevel Combinatorial Structure of the Capacitated Vertex Separator Problem

**Fabio Furini · Ivana Ljubić · Enrico Malaguti · Paolo Paronuzzi**  
*Operations Research* (2022) · [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2021.2110) · [PDF](https://fabiofurini.github.io/papers/J36_2022_OPRE_casting-light-hidden-bilevel-combinatorial-structure.pdf) · [Code](https://github.com/paoloparonuzzi/CVSP)

> **Main message.** A capacitated graph-separation problem can be viewed as a **bilevel interdiction problem**. This perspective leads to an exact integer-programming method strengthened by three families of inequalities with polynomial-time separation.

## 1. The optimization problem

Consider an undirected graph. The *Capacitated Vertex Separator Problem* (CVSP) asks us to remove a **minimum-cardinality set of vertices** such that the remaining graph admits a prescribed number of pairwise disconnected *shores*, each subject to a cardinality limit.

The problem is relevant both to **network vulnerability and protection**, where vertex deletion models disruptive interventions, and to **matrix decomposition**, where graph separation reflects structural partitioning.

The combinatorial difficulty lies in choosing the deleted vertices while ensuring that the surviving network has the required disconnected structure.

## 2. Key insight: turn separation into a leader–follower game

Rather than reasoning only in terms of which vertices should be removed, the paper makes the hidden **bilevel** structure explicit:

- **Leader:** chooses the vertices to interdict (remove).
- **Follower:** solves a combinatorial optimization problem on the graph left by that decision.

This *Stackelberg-game interpretation* provides a different way to encode the separation requirement and, crucially, to derive useful information about feasible interdiction decisions.

The bilevel formulation is not presented merely as an alternative description: it is the source of the algorithmic strengthening developed in the paper.

## 3. Main contributions

### A. A bilevel interpretation of CVSP

The paper identifies the leader–follower structure behind the capacitated vertex separator problem, bringing together ideas from graph separation and interdiction.

**Why it matters:** the follower's optimization problem gives a systematic lens through which to examine and strengthen the leader's decisions.

### B. An exact integer-programming framework in the natural variable space

The bilevel viewpoint supports an integer-programming formulation based on the original decisions, rather than relying on an explicit enumeration of possible graph partitions.

**Why it matters:** this gives a foundation for an exact approach that can be strengthened through inequalities.

### C. Three families of strengthening inequalities

The authors derive **three distinct families of valid inequalities** motivated by the bilevel structure and establish that each family can be **separated in polynomial time**.

**Why it matters:** polynomial-time separation makes these inequalities practical ingredients of an exact cutting-plane-based framework, not just theoretical descriptions.

### D. Extension to a min–max variant

The framework is also extended to a **min–max version** of the problem.

**Why it matters:** the underlying modelling idea applies beyond the original minimum-cardinality variant.

## 4. What the computational experiments show

The method is evaluated on benchmark instances from the literature and compared with existing algorithms.

The reported results support two conclusions:

1. The new exact method is **competitive with the state of the art** on the tested instances.
2. It **improves best-known results** on several difficult classes of instances.

These are the findings reported in the paper; they should not be read as a claim of universal dominance across all instance families.

## 5. Broader significance

The most transferable contribution is the **modelling strategy**: use a follower's combinatorial response to expose the structure of a graph modification problem, then translate that structure into inequalities suitable for exact optimization.

The authors discuss the possibility of extending the idea to other **vertex or edge deletion/insertion** and **graph partitioning** problems.
