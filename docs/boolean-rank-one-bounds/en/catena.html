G Chapter G

The complete chain

All the previous chapters can be summed up in a single line. Six relations connect seven quantities: five are equalities, and only one is an inequality.

In this chapter: the interactive chain, with the value of each link on the chosen example and the mathematical reason behind each arrow; then a second, purely algebraic proof of $\LB=M$, which goes through the marginal potentials and does not use matroids.

1. The chain

$$\begin{aligned}\Efull(\Gamma)\ &\overset{(1)}{=}\ z^*_{\mathrm{AWCD}}\ \overset{(2)}{\color{#b42318}{\boldsymbol{\ge}}}\ \LB\ \overset{(3)}{=}\ \val(R)-\gamma\\ &\overset{(4)}{=}\ w(F^*)-\gamma\ \overset{(5)}{=}\ M\ \overset{(6)}{=}\ \text{AWCD matching bound}\end{aligned}$$

The symbols, in brief. $\Efull(\Gamma)$ is the minimum $L^1$ error on the upper triangle (diagonal included) of a conic combination $H=\sum_U p_U X_U$ of factors $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$. $z^*_{\mathrm{AWCD}}$ is the value of Stage A of AWCD with a single root. $\gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_{ii}$ is the trace.

$\val(R)$ is the value of the one-sided relaxation $R$; $\LB$ is the lower bound derived from it. $F^*$ is the maximum-weight pseudoforest and $w(F^*)$ its weight. $M$ is the maximum matching of the gains $(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{vv})^+$, with $(t)^+=\max(t,0)$.

Key idea

The whole bottom block is a single number, seen from four sides: relaxation, matroid, matching, dual certificate. The only loss occurs in step (2): making the off-diagonal overestimate free.

Caution

Equal bounds do not mean exactness. On the $3\times3$ the four sides of the bottom block are all equal to $2$, but $\Efull=3$.

2. A second proof: the marginal potentials

Relations (4) and (5) go through the bicircular matroid. There is also a direct route from $R$ to the matching, using only changes of variables and LP duality.

The marginal potential of vertex $i$ is the total weight of the factors that contain it:

$$\alpha_i=\sum_{U\ni i}p_U=H_{ii}(p).$$

In $R$ the cost of the factors is $\sum_U|U|\,p_U=\sum_i\alpha_i$: it depends only on the marginals.

The chain of superlevel sets covers every pair as much as possible

Given $\alpha$, sort its distinct values $0<\theta_1<\dots<\theta_k$ and take the superlevel sets $S_t=\{v:\alpha_v\ge\theta_t\}$ with weight $\theta_t-\theta_{t-1}$ (and $\theta_0=0$). These sets are nested: a chain.

The chain has the same marginals $\alpha$ and covers the pair $\{i,j\}$ exactly $\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$. No $p$ with marginals $\alpha$ does better, because $H_{ij}\le\min(H_{ii},H_{jj})$.

Proof: coverage of the chain $=\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$

Vertex $v$ belongs to $S_t$ if and only if $\theta_t\le\alpha_v$. Hence its marginal in the chain is $\sum_{t:\,\theta_t\le\alpha_v}(\theta_t-\theta_{t-1})=\alpha_v$ (telescoping sum).

The pair $\{i,j\}$ lies in $S_t$ if and only if $\theta_t\le\alpha_i$ and $\theta_t\le\alpha_j$, i.e. $\theta_t\le\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$; the same telescoping sum gives $\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$.

For an arbitrary $p$, every $U\supseteq\{i,j\}$ contains both $i$ and $j$: hence $H_{ij}\le\alpha_i$ and $H_{ij}\le\alpha_j$.

The compact formulation

In $R$ the off-diagonal overestimate is free, so it pays to cover every pair as much as possible. It is therefore enough to choose $\alpha$ and take the chain. $R$ becomes an LP with $n+\binom n2$ variables:

$$\begin{aligned}\val(R)=\min\ &\sum_i\alpha_i+\sum_{i\lt j}q_{ij}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\alpha_i\ge\Gamma_{ii}\ \ (i\in V),\qquad \alpha_i+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij},\ \ \alpha_j+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}\ \ (i\lt j),\qquad q\ge0 .\end{aligned}$$

The constraint $\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}$ has split into two linear constraints, one per endpoint.

Change of variables: the diagonal excess

Set $\beta_i=\alpha_i-\Gamma_{ii}\ge0$, the excess coverage on the diagonal. Then $\sum_i\alpha_i=\sum_i\beta_i+\gamma$, and this $\gamma$ cancels the one in $\LB$:

$$\begin{aligned}\LB=\val(R)-\gamma=\min\ &\sum_i\beta_i+\sum_{i\lt j}q_{ij}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\beta_i+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{ii},\ \ \beta_j+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{jj}\ \ (i\lt j),\qquad \beta,q\ge0 .\end{aligned}$$

The dual is bipartite matching

Assign a dual variable $y_{ij,i}\ge0$ to the constraint of endpoint $i$ of the pair $\{i,j\}$ (and $y_{ij,j}$ to the other one); in general, $y_{e,v}$ is the variable of the pair formed by edge $e$ and endpoint $v\in e$. The columns of $\beta_v$ and $q_{ij}$ give the dual constraints:

$$\begin{aligned}\max\ &\sum_{i\lt j}\Bigl[(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{ii})\,y_{ij,i}+(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{jj})\,y_{ij,j}\Bigr]\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\underbrace{\sum_{e\ni v}y_{e,v}\le1}_{\text{column }\beta_v},\qquad \underbrace{y_{ij,i}+y_{ij,j}\le1}_{\text{column }q_{ij}},\qquad y\ge0 .\end{aligned}$$

This is fractional matching in the bipartite graph edges ↔ vertices: capacity 1 per vertex (from the $\beta$), capacity 1 per edge (from the $q$).

The constraint matrix is the incidence matrix of a bipartite graph, hence totally unimodular: every square submatrix has determinant $0$, $+1$ or $-1$. With integer right-hand sides the LP has an integral optimum: a true matching. Pairs with negative gain stay at zero, and the value is $M$.

Conclusion

$\LB=\val(R)-\gamma=M$, without going through matroids. The $y_{ij,v}=1$ at the optimum are exactly the pairs of the AWCD certificate of Chapter F: $+1$ on edge $\{i,j\}$, $-1$ on root edge $(r,v)$.

Key takeaways
  • $\Efull=z^*\ge\LB=\val(R)-\gamma=w(F^*)-\gamma=M=$ AWCD matching bound. The only inequality is (2).
  • The gap comes only from the off-diagonal overestimate, which $R$ makes free.
  • In $R$ only the marginals $\alpha$ matter: the chain of superlevel sets covers the pair $\{i,j\}$ exactly $\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$.
  • With $\beta_i=\alpha_i-\Gamma_{ii}$, the dual of $R$ is a bipartite matching, integral by total unimodularity.
