The two matroids are not the same

In the AWCD bordered graph every loop $\ell_i$ becomes an edge $(r,i)$ to the root $r$. It looks as if pseudoforests thereby turn into forests. They do not, and the two optimal values measure different things.

In this chapter: we compare the bicircular matroid and the graphic matroid, run them side by side on small graphs, and see why the value of the second is not a lower bound on the distance.

Two structures side by side

We start from the same matrix $\Gamma$ (symmetric, nonnegative, $n\times n$) and its trace $\gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_{ii}$. We build two weighted graphs and a matroid on each.

Bicircular matroid relaxation $R$

  • GraphVertices $V=\{1,\dots,n\}$, one loop $\ell_i$ per vertex with weight $\Gamma_{ii}$, edges $\{i,j\}$ with weight $\Gamma_{ij}$.
  • Independent setsPseudoforests: every component has at most one cycle (a loop counts as a cycle).
  • OriginLP dual of the one-sided relaxation $(R)$.
  • Optimum$F^*$, a maximum-weight pseudoforest (greedy): $w(F^*)=\val(R)$.
  • Bound$\LB=w(F^*)-\gamma\le z^*$.

Graphic matroid AWCD covering

  • GraphBordered graph: complete on $V\cup\{r\}$, root edges $(r,i)$ with weight $\Gamma_{ii}$, edges $\{i,j\}$ with weight $\Gamma_{ij}$.
  • Independent setsForests: no cycles.
  • OriginDual of the AWCD covering over all subsets, with cost $|C|-1$.
  • Optimum$T$, a maximum spanning forest (Kruskal): $\Zcov(w)=w(T)$.
  • Bound$\Zcov(w)\le z^*+f_2^*$.

Here $w(\cdot)$ is the total weight of a set of elements, $z^*=\Efull(\Gamma)$ is the optimal value of AWCD Stage A (the $L^1$ distance from the cone), and $f_2^*$ is the optimal value of Stage B. The precise definitions are in the box below.

Definition · covering relaxation and Stage B

Let $w$ be the weight vector of the bordered graph. The covering relaxation uses all subsets $C\subseteq V\cup\{r\}$ with $|C|\ge2$, not only those that contain $r$:

$$\Zcov(w)=\min\Bigl\{\textstyle\sum_{C}(|C|-1)\,x_C:\ \sum_{C\supseteq e}x_C\ge w_e\ \text{for every edge } e,\ \ x\ge0\Bigr\}.$$

Its dual is $\max\{w^\top y: y(E[C])\le|C|-1\ \forall C,\ y\ge0\}$, where $E[C]$ is the set of edges with both endpoints in $C$. This is the forest polytope, hence $\Zcov(w)=w(T)$.

$f_2^*$ is the minimum of $\sum_C(|C|-1)x_C=\tr H$ over the optimal solutions of Stage A.

Proposition 1 · forests and pseudoforests

Read each loop $\ell_i$ as the edge $(r,i)$. The forests of the bordered graph are exactly the pseudoforests in which every cycle is a loop. Hence $w(T)\le w(F^*)$.

The two matroids have the same rank $n$. For $n=2$ they coincide; for $n\ge3$ the triangle is a pseudoforest but not a forest.

Proof

Let $T$ be a forest of the bordered graph. Without $r$, the ordinary edges of $T$ form a forest on $V$. Each of its trees meets $r$ through at most one edge: two such edges would close a cycle through $r$. Read with loops, every component is a tree with at most one loop, that is, a pseudoforest.

Conversely, if every cycle of a pseudoforest is a loop, every component is a tree with at most one loop. Replacing the loop by $(r,i)$ attaches the tree to $r$ through a single edge: no cycle arises.

Rank: a pseudoforest has at most $n$ elements, and a forest on $n+1$ vertices has at most $n$ edges; the set of all loops (the star of $r$) attains both. For $n=2$ a cycle without loops would need three vertices.

Proposition 2 · a bound on distance plus trace

$\Zcov(w)\le z^*+f_2^*$. In general $\Zcov(w)$ is neither a lower bound nor an upper bound on $z^*$.

Proof

Take an optimal solution $(x,q^+,q^-)$ of Stage B: $Bx+q^+-q^-=w$, $\sum_e(q^+_e+q^-_e)=z^*$ and $\sum_C(|C|-1)x_C=f_2^*$. Here $B$ is the edge–anchored-clique incidence matrix, $q^+$ the under-coverage and $q^-$ the over-coverage.

Every anchored clique is allowed in the covering. For each edge $e$ we add the set $C=e$ (cost $1$) with weight $q^+_e$. The coverage of $e$ becomes $(Bx)_e+q^+_e=w_e+q^-_e\ge w_e$. The cost is $f_2^*+\sum_e q^+_e\le f_2^*+z^*$.

The figure below shows a case with $\Zcov\lt z^*$ ($t=0$) and one with $\Zcov\gt z^*$ ($t=\tfrac12$).

Two greedy algorithms side by side

We use the family $\Gamma_{ij}=1$ for $i\ne j$ and $\Gamma_{ii}=t$, on three vertices. Both algorithms receive the same order: nonincreasing weight, with ties broken in favour of loops (that is, root edges).

On the left, the bicircular greedy rejects an element only if it would create a second cycle. On the right, Kruskal rejects every edge that closes a cycle.

The minimal counterexample: $\Gamma_{ii}=0$, $\Gamma_{ij}=1$

With $t=0$ only the three edges of the triangle remain. The bicircular greedy takes all of them, because the triangle is a single cycle: $w(F^*)=3$. Kruskal stops at two edges: $w(T)=2$.

Here $\gamma=0$, so $\LB=3$. And indeed $\Efull(\Gamma)=3$: the best reconstruction is $H=0$, which is off by $1$ on each of the three pairs.

Caution

$w(T)=2\lt 3=w(F^*)$ does not mean “a weaker bound on the same quantity”. $w(F^*)-\gamma$ bounds $z^*$ from below; $w(T)$ bounds $z^*+f_2^*$ from above. With $t=\tfrac12$: $w(T)=2.5$ exceeds $z^*=1.5$.

Large diagonal: the forest is the star of the root

With $t=5$ the loops outweigh the edges. The greedy takes $\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3$; Kruskal takes the star $(r,1),(r,2),(r,3)$. It is the same set, independent in both matroids: $w(F^*)=w(T)=\gamma=15$.

But $\Gamma=3\sum_i X_{\{i\}}+\sum_{i\lt j}X_{\{i,j\}}$ lies in the cone, where $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$ and $\one_U$ is the indicator vector of $U$. Hence $z^*=0$ and $\Zcov=15=z^*+f_2^*$: the whole value comes from the trace. The pseudoforest bound instead gives $\LB=15-15=0$.

Key idea

In the bordered graph, adding the root does not turn the bicircular matroid into the graphic one. The correspondence $\ell_i\leftrightarrow(r,i)$ preserves elements and rank, but the bicircular matroid also admits cycles made only of ordinary edges. When the diagonal dominates, the values coincide because of the weights, not because of the matroids.

Key takeaways
  • Bicircular: it arises from the dual of $(R)$ and gives $\LB=w(F^*)-\gamma\le z^*$.
  • Graphic: it arises from the AWCD covering with cost $|C|-1$ and gives $\Zcov(w)=w(T)\le z^*+f_2^*$. It is not the dual of the anchored Stage A.
  • Under $\ell_i\leftrightarrow(r,i)$ every forest is a pseudoforest; the triangle shows that the converse fails.
  • The two values do not bound the same quantity: at $t=0$, $w(T)=2\lt 3=\Efull$; at $t=5$, $w(T)=15\gt 0=z^*$.
