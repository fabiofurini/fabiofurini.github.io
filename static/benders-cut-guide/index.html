Stronger Benders cuts, step by step

One graph, one root, one requested destination: understand exactly where the cut coefficients come from.

The paper's running graph

We use the same five-node, ten-arc directed network as in the big-M guide. Here we choose root 2, requested destination 3, unit interdiction costs for this teaching example, and budget 1. Arc labels show the original lengths (the paper's other figures also show its original interdiction costs). The specific unit-cost experiment here is illustrative, not the paper's weighted-budget running example.

Every diagram repeats the complete graph; arcs in opposite directions follow separate curved lanes. Blue highlights the route being discussed; red dashed indicates the deleted arc.

1Original shortest path

The selected follower path is 2 → 5 → 3, of total length L = 5. Both its arcs have flow value one; all other arcs have flow value zero for this requested pair.

θ₂ ≤ 5 + γ₂₅ x₂₅ + γ₅₃ x₅₃

2Interdict arc (2,5)

After deleting (2,5), the path originally chosen by the follower disappears. The shortest surviving alternative is 2 → 1 → 5 → 3, of length 10.

γ₂₅ ≥ 10 − 5 = 5

3Interdict arc (5,3)

After deleting (5,3), the shortest surviving alternative is 2 → 5 → 4 → 3, of length 9.

γ₅₃ ≥ 9 − 5 = 4

4Both coefficients in the strengthened cut

Because the budget permits deleting at most one arc, these singleton increases yield the componentwise smallest nonnegative coefficients for this selected cut.

θ₂ ≤ 5 + 5x₂₅ + 4x₅₃
The ordinary Benders inequality uses γₐ = M(a,2) ỹₐ₂. If the chosen valid big-M values are larger than 5 and 4, this cut-specific inequality is tighter. It gives the same required upper bound for each feasible interdiction in this budget-one example.

Why this is a lifting, not just a recalculation

The general cut-specific validity condition in the v2 paper is:

∑a∈I γₐ ≥ τK(w,I) − L   for every feasible I.

For budget greater than one, separate singleton increases need not be jointly valid: deleting two arcs can have a synergistic effect on residual distances. Then the coefficients must satisfy the inequalities for all feasible interdiction sets, or use the paper's safe tree-coefficient rule.

In this example the standard cost assumptions of the running paper example have been replaced with unit interdiction costs to make the single-arc lifting transparent. The directed graph and arc lengths are unchanged.
