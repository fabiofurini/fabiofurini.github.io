PAPER GUIDE · MATHEMATICAL PROGRAMMING · 2022

How to optimize concave utility over millions of covering relationships

Double-hypograph decomposition separates the nonlinear utility from the covering structure—and projects out the item variables.

Stefano Coniglio · Fabio Furini · Ivana Ljubić

IN THIS GUIDEThe problemThe decompositionWhat is newThe resultsRead more
THE MAIN CONTRIBUTION

Exploit two independent structures—the concavity of the utility function and the set-union covering operator—to obtain an exact MILP reformulation without explicit variables for every covered item.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Which facilities should we select when coverage has diminishing returns?

Choose a subset of metaitems (for example, facilities) under a cardinality constraint. Selecting metaitems covers a union of items (for example, customers); the objective is the expected value of a concave utility of the covered items' scenario-dependent values.

The difficulty is both combinatorial (which items are covered by a set union?) and nonlinear (how to represent the concave objective exactly?). The formulation can involve vastly more items than metaitems.

02 / THE INSIGHT

Two hypographs, two sources of structure

01Utility

Exact outer approximation handles the concave function.

02Coverage

Use either submodular cuts or Benders cuts for the set-union operator.

03Projection

Remove the large family of item-associated variables from the resulting model.

The paper calls this a double-hypograph decomposition. Its principal benefit is the ability to model problems where the number of covered items is several orders of magnitude larger than the number of selectable metaitems.

03 / THE CONTRIBUTIONS

More than a new reformulation

New exact reformulations

Outer approximation combined with submodular cuts (OA+SC) or Benders cuts (OA+BC).

Provable separation complexity

Under constant-time utility evaluation, OA+BC inequalities are separable in linear time, including at fractional points.

Theory of inequality strength

Formal comparisons of the families, including results on relative strength.

Generalization beyond monotonicity

An extension for concave differentiable utility functions that need not be increasing.

See Sections 3–5 and the appendix for the derivations, proofs and counterexamples.

04 / COMPUTATIONAL RESULTS

Large item sets become manageable

The computational experiments compare branch-and-cut approaches based on the different reformulations, as well as classical and global-optimization alternatives. The approach combining outer approximation with Benders cuts is reported as the strongest in the experimental comparison.

05 / WHY READ THE PAPER?

A decomposition blueprint for nonlinear covering models

The central lesson is that the structure of a nonlinear objective and the structure of a combinatorial mapping need not be treated together. Separating the two can lead to compact, strong, and exactly solvable formulations.
