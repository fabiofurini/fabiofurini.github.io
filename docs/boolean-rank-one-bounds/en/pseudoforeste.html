Primal and dual: the relaxation $R$ and pseudoforests

The relaxation $(R)$ buys factors $X_U$ at the price of their trace. Its dual $(P)$ selects elements of a graph with one loop per vertex. The integer solutions of $(P)$ are exactly the pseudoforests.

In this chapter: we read $(R)$ and $(P)$ side by side; we see why the greedy algorithm finds the optimal pseudoforest $F^*$; with component peeling we build an optimum of $(R)$ with the same value; finally we pass from a laminar certificate to a chain.
Notation
$V=\{1,\dots,n\}$ is the vertex set and $\Gamma$ is a symmetric nonnegative $n\times n$ matrix; $\gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_{ii}$ is its trace. For $\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V$, $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$ is the Boolean rank-one factor with $(X_U)_{ij}=1$ if and only if $i,j\in U$, and $p_U\ge 0$ is its weight. The associated graph is complete on $V$, with a loop $\ell_i$ (an edge from $i$ to $i$) at every vertex: the loops and the edges $\{i,j\}$ are the elements, with weights $w_{\ell_i}=\Gamma_{ii}$ and $w_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}$. For a set $F$ of elements, $w(F)=\sum_{e\in F}w_e$.

1. Two LPs, face to face

An LP (linear program) optimizes a linear function subject to linear constraints; its dual swaps variables and constraints. Here $q_{ij}\ge 0$ is the underestimate paid on cell $(i,j)$, and $x_e\ge 0$ is the dual variable of element $e$. Pick a pair to see it highlighted.

$(R)$ — covering, minimum

$$\begin{aligned} \min\ & \sum_{U}|U|\,\htmlClass{cc cc-p}{p_U}+\sum_{i<j}\htmlClass{cc cc-q}{q_{ij}}\\[2pt] \text{s.t. }\ & \htmlClass{cc cc-d}{\textstyle\sum_{U\ni i}p_U\ge\Gamma_{ii}}\quad (i\in V)\\ & \htmlClass{cc cc-o}{\textstyle\sum_{U\supseteq\{i,j\}}p_U+q_{ij}\ge\Gamma_{ij}}\quad (i<j)\\ & p,\ q\ \ge 0 \end{aligned}$$

$(P)$ — packing, maximum

$$\begin{aligned} \max\ & \sum_{i}\Gamma_{ii}\,\htmlClass{cc cc-d}{x_{\ell_i}}+\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_{ij}\,\htmlClass{cc cc-o}{x_{ij}}\\[2pt] \text{s.t. }\ & \htmlClass{cc cc-p}{x(E[U])\le |U|}\quad (U\neq\emptyset)\\ & \htmlClass{cc cc-q}{x_{ij}\le 1}\quad (i<j)\\ & x\ \ge 0 \end{aligned}$$

Here $E[U]$ is the set of elements (loops and edges) with all endpoints in $U$, and $x(E[U])=\sum_{e\in E[U]}x_e$. $\val(\cdot)$ denotes the optimal value. $(R)$ is feasible and $(P)$ is bounded, hence $\val(R)=\val(P)$.

2. The integer solutions of $(P)$: pseudoforests

Pseudoforest
A set $F$ of loops and edges is a pseudoforest if every connected component contains at most one cycle. A loop is a cycle. A matroid is a family of “independent” sets that is closed under taking subsets and has the exchange property; pseudoforests are the independent sets of the bicircular matroid.
Proposition
For a set $F$ of elements: $|F\cap E[U]|\le |U|$ for every $U\neq\emptyset$ if and only if every component of $F$ has at most one cycle.
Proof

A component with $v$ vertices and $e$ elements (loops included) has $e-v+1$ independent cycles: it is a tree if $e=v-1$, has exactly one cycle if $e=v$, and has at least two if $e\ge v+1$.

($\Leftarrow$) Every component of $F\cap E[U]$ lies inside a component of $F$, so it has at most one cycle and at most as many elements as vertices. Summing over the components, all with vertices in $U$: $|F\cap E[U]|\le|U|$.

($\Rightarrow$) If a component $K$ has two cycles, then $e\ge v+1$ and the constraint for $U=V(K)$ is violated.

Polytope. The bicircular rank of a set $A$ is $\sum_K\min(e_K,v_K)$ over its components. If $x$ satisfies $(P)$: for a cyclic component $x(A_K)\le x(E[V(K)])\le v_K$; for a tree $x(A_K)\le e_K$ because $x_e\le 1$. Hence $x(A)\le r(A)$ for every $A$, and the polytope of $(P)$ is the independence polytope of the matroid (Edmonds). Its vertices are the pseudoforests: $\val(P)=w(F^*)$.

3. The greedy algorithm finds $F^*$

The greedy algorithm scans the elements by decreasing weight and accepts an element if the set remains a pseudoforest. On a matroid with nonnegative weights it is optimal: it returns $F^*$, the maximum-weight pseudoforest.

It suffices to record, for each component, whether it already has a cycle. A loop or an edge inside a component closes a cycle: it needs an acyclic component. An edge between two components is forbidden only if both already have a cycle.

4. Component peeling: an optimum of $(R)$

To certify $F^*$ we need a solution of $(R)$ with the same value. Component peeling builds it by peeling the pseudoforest level by level.

Algorithm
  1. Augmentation. Start from $\bar F=F^*$. Scan the elements of positive weight in weight order and add every edge that joins two different components of $\bar F$. The greedy algorithm rejected these edges because both components already had a cycle.
  2. Component with a cycle. Let $V_C$ be its vertex set. Pay a common level $p_{V_C}=\delta$, the minimum residual in $C$ (residual = the part of the weight not yet covered), and subtract $\delta$ from all its elements.
  3. Tree component. Pay each edge with $q_{ij}$ equal to its residual.
Why this suffices
$(p,q)$ is feasible and has value $w(F^*)$. But $w(F^*)\le\val(P)=\val(R)\le$ value of $(p,q)$: all of these are equal, and the lower bound on the error is $\LB=\val(R)-\gamma=w(F^*)-\gamma$.

5. Laminar or chain

The sets produced by peeling are vertex sets of components of ever smaller graphs. Hence any two of them are either disjoint or nested: the family is laminar, with at most $2n-1$ sets.

The marginal of vertex $v$, $\alpha_v=\sum_{U\ni v}p_U$, is the total coverage of the diagonal cell $(v,v)$. The marginals allow us to replace the family with a chain of at most $n$ nested sets, the superlevel sets:

$$S_k=\{v:\alpha_v\ge\theta_k\},\qquad p'_{S_k}=\theta_k-\theta_{k-1},$$

where $\theta_1<\theta_2<\dots$ are the distinct levels, i.e. the distinct positive values of the marginals, $\theta_0=0$, and $p'$ is the new weight vector.

Why the chain has the same value (proof and pair coverages)

For every $v$ the telescoping sum gives $\sum_{k:\,v\in S_k}p'_{S_k}=\alpha_v$: the same diagonal constraints and the same cost $\sum_U|U|p_U=\sum_v\alpha_v$.

A pair $i<j$ is covered by the chain to the amount $\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$, whereas $p$ covers it by $\sum_{U\supseteq\{i,j\}}p_U\le\min(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$. The coverage does not decrease, so the same $q$ remains feasible.

Key takeaways
  • $(R)$ covers, $(P)$ packs: $p_U\leftrightarrow x(E[U])\le|U|$ and $q_{ij}\leftrightarrow x_{ij}\le 1$.
  • The integer solutions of $(P)$ are the pseudoforests: the greedy algorithm finds $F^*$.
  • Peeling yields $(p,q)$ with value $w(F^*)$, hence $\val(R)=w(F^*)$.
  • Laminar certificate ($\le 2n-1$ sets) or chain ($\le n$ nested sets): same value.
