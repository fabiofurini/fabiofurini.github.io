The AWCD matching bound

In the AWCD manuscript (Optimization over Anchored Clique Cones, the work led by Giovanni Rinaldi) the lower bound does not come from a relaxation: solutions of the dual are built by hand. The most natural family is indexed by matchings, and its best value is the number $M$ of Chapter E.

In this chapter: the Stage A dual in two forms; how a valid inequality becomes a certificate; why certificates can only be added along a matching; why the result is the same matching as for the pseudoforest; and why it is not the whole dual.

1. The Stage A dual

The bordered graph has vertices $V=\{1,\dots,n\}$ plus a root $r$. The root edge $(r,i)$ has weight $w_{ri}=\Gamma_{ii}$ (diagonal); the ordinary edge $\{i,j\}$ has weight $w_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}$ (off-diagonal).

$\K(G,\{r\})$ is the family of anchored cliques $C=\{r\}\cup U$, with $\emptyset\ne U\subseteq V$; $E(C)$ is the set of their edges. Stage A of AWCD computes $z^*$, the minimum $L^1$ distance between $w$ and the conic combinations of anchored cliques; the equivalence theorem states $z^*=\Efull(\Gamma)$.

Definition · $(D_A)$, the LP dual of Stage A

One variable $y_e$ for each edge $e$ of the bordered graph:

$$\begin{aligned}(D_A)\qquad \max\ &\sum_{e} w_e\,y_e\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\sum_{e\in E(C)} y_e\le 0\quad \forall\, C\in\K(G,\{r\}),\\ &-1\le y_e\le 1 .\end{aligned}$$

The optimal value is $z^*=\Efull(\Gamma)$.

The same problem in matrix coordinates: the root edge $(r,i)$ becomes the diagonal cell $(i,i)$, and the edge $\{i,j\}$ the cell $(i,j)$ with $i\lt j$. The edges of $C=\{r\}\cup U$ are the upper-triangle cells with both indices in $U$:

$$\begin{aligned}\max\ &\sum_{i\le j}\Gamma_{ij}\,y_{ij}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\sum_{i\le j,\ i,j\in U} y_{ij}\le 0\quad \forall\,U\neq\emptyset,\\ &-1\le y_{ij}\le 1 .\end{aligned}$$
Why every feasible $y$ gives a lower bound (weak duality)

Stage A is $\min\{e^\top q^++e^\top q^-:\ Bx+q^+-q^-=w,\ x,q^\pm\ge 0\}$: $x_C\ge0$ is the weight of clique $C$, column $C$ of $B$ is the indicator of $E(C)$, $q^+$ and $q^-$ are the under- and over-coverage, and $e$ is the all-ones vector. Multiplying the constraints by $y$:

$$w^\top y=\sum_C x_C\underbrace{\sum_{e\in E(C)}y_e}_{\le 0}+\sum_e q^+_e\underbrace{y_e}_{\le 1}-\sum_e q^-_e\underbrace{y_e}_{\ge -1}\ \le\ e^\top q^++e^\top q^- .$$

Hence $w^\top y\le z^*$ for every feasible $y$; strong duality says that the maximum is exactly $z^*$.

Key idea

The constraint for $U$ reads $\sum_{i\le j}y_{ij}(X_U)_{ij}\le 0$, where $X_U=\one_U\one_U^\top$ is the factor of $U$. Since $\COR(n)$, the cone generated by the factors, consists of their conic combinations, a solution of $(D_A)$ is a valid inequality for $\COR(n)$, with coefficients in $[-1,1]$.

2. From $H_{ij}\le H_{ii}$ to a certificate

If $i,j\in U$ then $i\in U$, so $(X_U)_{ij}\le(X_U)_{ii}$. Summing with weights $p_U\ge0$, every $H=\sum_U p_UX_U$ in $\COR(n)$ satisfies $H_{ij}\le H_{ii}$ (and $H_{ij}\le H_{jj}$).

The coefficients of $H_{ij}-H_{ii}\le 0$ are $+1$ in $(i,j)$ and $-1$ in $(i,i)$. In the bordered graph, assigning edge $\{i,j\}$ to vertex $i$ puts $y=+1$ on edge $\{i,j\}$ and $y=-1$ on the root edge $(r,i)$.

On a clique $C=\{r\}\cup U$ the sum is $[\,i,j\in U\,]-[\,i\in U\,]$, where $[\cdot]$ is 1 if the condition holds and 0 otherwise. It equals $0$ or $-1$, never $+1$. Try it: click the vertices to change $U$.

3. Adding certificates: a matching is needed

Take a set $F$ of ordinary edges and an edge→endpoint assignment $\varphi$, that is, $\varphi(e)\in e$ for every $e\in F$. With $\chi^{\{e\}}$, the vector equal to 1 on edge $e$ and 0 elsewhere, we define

$$y^{F,\varphi}=\sum_{e\in F}\bigl(\chi^{\{e\}}-\chi^{\{r\varphi(e)\}}\bigr).$$

Each term satisfies the clique constraints, and therefore so does the sum. Only the box $-1\le y\le 1$ can break:

Edge reused

Using the same edge $\{i,j\}$ twice gives $y_{ij}=2\gt 1$. Capacity 1 on the edges.

Vertex reused

Two edges assigned to the same $v$ give $y_{rv}=-2\lt -1$. Capacity 1 on the vertices: $\varphi$ injective.

With capacity 1 on both sides, the pairs $(e,\varphi(e))$ form a matching in the bipartite graph edges ↔ vertices. The value of the certificate is the sum of the gains:

$$w^\top y^{F,\varphi}=\sum_{e=\{i,j\}\in F}\bigl(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{\varphi(e)\varphi(e)}\bigr).$$
Proposition · AWCD matching bound

If $\varphi$ is injective, $y^{F,\varphi}$ is feasible for $(D_A)$, hence $\Efull(\Gamma)\ge\sum_{e\in F}(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{\varphi(e)\varphi(e)})$. The best one is worth $M$, the maximum matching with weights $(\Gamma_{ij}-\Gamma_{vv})^+$, where $(t)^+=\max(t,0)$.

What about fractional combinations?

One can assign half of an edge to $i$ and half to $j$: $y_{ij}=1$, $y_{ii}=y_{jj}=-\tfrac12$ is feasible. But then the pair weights form a fractional matching, and the bipartite matching polytope has integral vertices: the maximum is still $M$.

5. Only a family, not the whole dual

Caution

The certificates $y^{F,\varphi}$ have $+1$ only on ordinary edges and $-1$ only on root edges. They are a particular family of solutions of $(D_A)$: the full dual is worth $\Efull(\Gamma)$, which can be larger than $M$.

On the $3\times3$ with $\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&1&0\\2&0&1\end{pmatrix}$ the matching gives $2$; the certificate on the right is worth $3=\Efull(\Gamma)$.

Key takeaways
  • A solution of $(D_A)$ is a valid inequality for $\COR(n)$; its value is a lower bound on $\Efull$.
  • $H_{ij}\le H_{ii}$ becomes “$+1$ on edge $\{i,j\}$, $-1$ on root edge $(r,i)$”.
  • Adding certificates breaks only the box: capacity 1 on edges and vertices, i.e. a matching. The best one is worth $M$.
  • It is the same matching as for the pseudoforest, but only part of the dual.
